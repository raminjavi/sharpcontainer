The rules

Sharps rules for Canadian labs, in plain words

Biomedical waste is regulated provincially, and transport is regulated federally. Here is what the main guidance says about the container. Your biosafety officer and your provincial ministry have the final word.

1

Ontario Guideline C-4. Sharps are a category of biomedical waste. They go into puncture-resistant, leak-proof containers with a biohazard symbol and a yellow colour/label, are segregated at the point of generation, and leave the site only with a carrier holding a waste management system ECA.

Management of Biomedical Waste in Ontario · ontario.ca

2

Other provinces have their own rules, e.g. BC hospital/biomedical waste guidance under the Environmental Management Act; Québec's Règlement sur les déchets biomédicaux (Q-2, r.12); Alberta's waste control regulation and AHS guidance. Most are modelled on the CCME Guidelines for the Management of Biomedical Waste in Canada (1992).

3

Containment labs. The Canadian Biosafety Standard (PHAC) requires sharps to be discarded in puncture-resistant containers at the point of use.

Canadian Biosafety Standard · canada.ca

4

Transport (TDG). Off-site shipment of biomedical waste falls under the federal Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations (Class 6.2). Packing for transport is the responsibility of the shipper and the licensed carrier.

TDG Regulations · tc.canada.ca
